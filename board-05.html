Two numbers doing two different jobs

Backlog is the remediation workload. It falls slowly and its size is mostly a legacy artefact — a team inheriting 24,000 defects is not a team that created them. Introduced in the last 30 days is the governance metric: it answers whether the problem is still being made. A unit cleaning hard while its pipeline refills the bucket looks excellent on backlog and terrible here, which is the correct reading.

Attribution is the whole point, and it works only because of BR-1

2,168 of the 5,502 new defects trace to one run of one integration on one day. Without Introduced_By_Run__c written at load time, that number is simply "Registrar has a data problem" — which is false, unactionable, and unfair to the people who would be asked to fix it. Provenance is what turns a backlog into a cause.

What happens without it

Some defects genuinely cannot or should not be fixed. Without a reviewed-and-accepted state carrying a reviewer, a reason and a re-review date, the same few hundred unfixable records appear on every monthly report forever. Staff learn within two cycles that the list contains known noise, stop reading it carefully, and then miss the new items among the old. The board is dead long before anyone declares it dead. It costs almost nothing to build now and is close to unrecoverable once trust has gone.
