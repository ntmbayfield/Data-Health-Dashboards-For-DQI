How the score is composed — and where it lies
30 reliability  ·  30 completeness  ·  25 introduction rate  ·  15 responsiveness

Reliability — successful-run rate, staleness, and attestation currency across the unit's integrations. The only component a unit controls without touching a record. Completeness — share of records attributed to the unit with no open S1 defect. Introduction rate — new S1 defects per 1,000 records touched in 30 days, volume-normalised so the largest unit is not automatically the worst. Responsiveness — open S1 defects aged over 30 days as a share of the total, which separates a unit working a big inherited backlog from one ignoring a small backlog.

Read this before showing the letter grade to anyone senior

Registrar scores F and Events D, and neither has a data entry problem. Both run bursty pipelines that introduced thousands of defects in a single afternoon — one semiannual graduate load and one broken matching key. The score is measuring that correctly. A leader reading only the letter will conclude something false about the people in those units, and will be difficult to talk out of it afterwards.

Three rules follow. Always render the four components beside the composite, so the number can be interrogated in the same glance it is read. Never publish a cross-unit ranking before every unit has had a worklist and one quiet cycle to act on it. And accept the residual distortion: volume normalisation softens the burst penalty but does not remove it, because a thousand defects created in an afternoon really are a thousand defects. If the score cannot be shown with its components, do not show it.

INT-004 · BR-3 · set 12 Aug by Alumni Rel. lead
Why alerts, not a metric

Board 02 tells the central team where the risk is. This board has to tell a unit what to do on a Monday morning. Every alert names the integration, the count, the deadline and the next action, and opens a worklist of actual records with names on them.

The failure mode this avoids

A unit handed a percentage it cannot act on disengages within two cycles — and it disengages correctly, because a number with no worklist behind it is not information, it is judgement. That is the single most common reason a governance dashboard is built, admired once, and never opened again.
