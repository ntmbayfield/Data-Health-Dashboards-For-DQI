How "unattested change" is detected
FLAG WHEN  Mapping_Last_Modified__c > Mapping_Last_Attested__c

Two independent dates, deliberately. Modified comes from Omatic automatically. Attested is entered by a human who opened the live mapping, compared it to the documented spec, and recorded the version number and the review method used — visual check, sample record trace, or full sandbox round-trip. A checkbox gets clicked reflexively; a form asking which version and which method does not.

What one date alone would miss

Tracking only "last modified" inverts the signal. A mapping changed last week reads as fresh and current when it is in fact unreviewed. A mapping untouched for three years reads as neglected when it may be perfectly correct. The two states are indistinguishable with one date, so the check silently does nothing. INT-006 is the case in point: attested 9 May, modified 11 May, then used to load 4,102 graduates on 26 May. The mapping that ran was not the mapping that was reviewed, and every conventional report would have shown a recently reviewed, recently updated, healthy integration.

How multi-writer conflict is detected
STATIC ANALYSIS  COUNT(Field_Mapping__c WHERE Target_Field = X AND writes) > 1

Derived from configuration on every mapping change, not from data at runtime.

Why nothing at runtime catches this

Every write succeeds. There is no error, no rejection, no warning — the last integration to run simply wins. Five pipelines write the email opt-out field, so a MarComm unsubscribe can be reversed by the Events or Registrar sync within hours, and the system's own logs will show two clean successful runs. It is visible only from outside the org, at the moment someone who unsubscribed receives another email — which is also the moment it stops being a data quality issue and becomes a compliance one. Consolidating the two shared vendor accounts removed writers from four rows; the opt-out row is unchanged and remains the highest-severity finding in the register.
